The science behind the model
  1. Auxin (IAA) is made in the shoot tip and moves toward the base by polar transport. In the slightly acidic cell wall, auxin is uncharged (IAAH) and diffuses into cells. In the neutral cytoplasm it loses a proton and becomes IAA⁻, which is trapped until PIN efflux carriers pump it out.
  2. The position of PIN carriers sets the direction of flow. PINs on the basal (lower) membrane send auxin downward. When the tip detects light from one side (phototropins) or gravity (amyloplasts settling in statocytes), PIN proteins relocate to the membranes facing the shaded or lower side, so auxin builds up there.
  3. In shoots, auxin promotes cell elongation (acid growth). Auxin activates proton pumps that move H⁺ into the cell wall. The lower pH activates expansins, which loosen the bonds between cellulose microfibrils, and turgor pressure stretches the cell.
  4. Differential growth causes the bend. The side with more auxin elongates faster, so a shoot curves toward light and away from the pull of gravity.
  5. Roots are far more sensitive. The concentration that stimulates shoot cells inhibits root cells. Auxin still collects on the lower side of a horizontal root, but there it slows elongation, so the upper side grows faster and the root curves down.
What the model simplifies
  • Auxin is in relative units. A value of 1 is the normal level in a vertical stem. The molar values on the growth graph are approximate.
  • The stem is drawn as two halves (left and right). A real stem is three-dimensional.
  • In the phototropism section, gravity is switched off so that light is the only stimulus. In a real plant both act at once.
  • Growth rates, pH values and timing are tuned to be realistic in direction and rough scale, not to match one species exactly.
  • Auxin trapped above an impermeable barrier is slowly inactivated in the model, as happens through conjugation and oxidation in real tissue.
